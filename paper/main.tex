\title{Error Propagation in Cascaded Speech Translation under Acoustic Channel
Degradations: A Black-Box Case Study of Commercial Speech Intelligence APIs}

\author[addr1]{Chang Tan}  % single-anonymised review: author identity visible to reviewers
\address[addr1]{Dalian University of Technology, Dalian, China}

\begin{abstract}
Cloud speech services are consumed as black boxes, yet the acoustic channels
feeding them are rarely clean. CascadeBench is a fully seeded black-box
benchmark measuring how fourteen degradation conditions from seven acoustic
families propagate through commercial speech APIs: five recognition systems
in three product classes of one provider, three cascaded and two end-to-end
translation paths, over a 480-sentence bilingual corpus with an eight-level
babble sweep from 20 to $-10$~dB. Recognition error rises
as a knee-shaped function of noise: the audio-LLM systems tolerate 2.2 to
2.5~dB (Mandarin) and 4.4 to 4.8~dB (English) more noise than the
specialised streaming system before error reaches ten percent, then
collapse below 0~dB into insertion-heavy hallucination above 100
percent character error. Cascade loss follows an empirical pass-through of
0.4 to 0.5 BLEU points per percentage point of recognition error, which
survives re-fitting on a second vendor's translation engine. A
150-utterance real-speech validation with a six-level real-babble ladder
reproduces the knee structure and hallucination regime, shows rooms with
measured impulse responses far milder than the synthetic stress case, and
bounds the audio-LLM advantage to the near-knee regime. End-to-end
translation is not automatically more robust: the omni-modal path is the
most fragile of the end-to-end paths and degrades more than the
LLM-frontend cascades in point estimate, with a reference-dependent
margin. Three-reference scoring, with one reference family-disjoint from
every system, shows that relative-loss rankings are stable across
references except for a sub-point inversion under the same-engine
reference. The recipe, corpus, degraded audio, and 43{,}046 logged API
responses are released.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and code availability}
The complete benchmark is publicly released at
\url{https://github.com/43aquarius/CascadeBench-Paper-AppliedAcoustics}:
the sentence corpus, the seeded perturbation implementations, the full
degraded audio tree, all per-sentence model transcripts and translations
(43{,}046 logged API responses), the analysis scripts, and the LaTeX source
of this manuscript. Every number and figure in the paper is regenerated
from the released logs by the released analysis scripts. Extended numeric
tables (complete pairwise significance results, severity-sweep numerics,
refusal tabulations, and the complete real-speech, Whisper, measured-RIR,
and second-backend results) are provided as supplementary material.
Because the
journal operates single-anonymised review, in which author identity is
visible to reviewers, the public link is given directly; an anonymised
snapshot is additionally provided with the submission.
