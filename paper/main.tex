\title{Error Propagation in Cascaded Speech Translation under Acoustic Channel
Degradations: A Black-Box Case Study of Commercial Speech Intelligence APIs}

\author[addr1]{Chang Tan\corref{cor1}}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\ead{aquars43@foxmail.com}
\address[addr1]{Dalian University of Technology, Dalian 116024, China}

\begin{abstract}
Cloud speech services are consumed as black boxes, yet the acoustic channels
feeding them are rarely clean. CascadeBench is a fully seeded black-box
benchmark measuring how fourteen degradation conditions from seven acoustic
families propagate through commercial speech APIs: five recognition systems
in three product classes of one provider, three cascaded and two end-to-end
translation paths, over a 480-sentence bilingual corpus with an eight-level babble
sweep from 20 to $-10$~dB. Recognition error rises
as a knee-shaped function of noise: the audio-LLM systems tolerate 2.2 to
2.5~dB (Mandarin) and 4.4 to 4.8~dB (English) more noise than the
specialised streaming system before error reaches ten percent, then
collapsing below 0~dB into insertion-heavy hallucination above 100
percent character error. Cascade loss follows an empirical pass-through of
0.4 to 0.5
BLEU points per percentage point of recognition error (point estimates
0.44 to 0.54; condition-level 95\% intervals spanning roughly 0.2 to 0.7
across the three cascades). A 150-utterance real-speech replication reproduces the knee
structure and hallucination regime, shows real-room reverberation far milder than the synthetic
stress case, and bounds the audio-LLM advantage to the near-knee regime:
at and below 0~dB real babble Qwen3-ASR errs more than the specialist
streamer. End-to-end translation is not automatically more robust: under
0~dB babble the omni-modal path loses a third of its clean BLEU score on
the point estimate, more than a well-frontended cascade (a paired
contrast whose significance is reference-dependent), while a streaming
end-to-end service is the most resilient path measured on synthetic
speech. A three-reference protocol (same-vendor, oracle, and a
family-disjoint GLM reference) exposes machine-reference scoring bias that
can invert rankings, while the relative-loss ordering of the translation
paths is stable across all three references. Hold-out measurements with
no vendor component in common --- an open-source Whisper recogniser run
locally, and a cascade re-built on a GLM translation backend --- replicate
the knee-and-collapse structure, the language-asymmetric hallucination
regime, and the cascade relative-loss band, and a measured-RIR reverb
condition calibrates the synthetic stress case against real rooms. The
recipe, corpus, degraded audio, and 41{,}854 logged vendor API responses
(plus 1{,}792 non-vendor calls) are released.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and code availability}
The complete benchmark is publicly released at
\url{https://github.com/43aquarius/CascadeBench-Paper-AppliedAcoustics}:
the sentence corpus, the seeded perturbation implementations, the full
degraded audio tree, all per-sentence model transcripts and translations
(38{,}494 logged API responses), the analysis scripts, and the LaTeX source
of this manuscript. Every number and figure in the paper is regenerated
from the released logs by the released analysis scripts. Because the
journal operates single-anonymised review, in which author identity is
visible to reviewers, the public link is given directly; an anonymised
snapshot is additionally provided with the submission.
