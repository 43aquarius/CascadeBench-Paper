\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This paper introduced CascadeBench, a black-box benchmark that measures how
acoustic channel degradations propagate through commercial speech
recognition and translation services, and reported a complete measurement
over a controlled bilingual corpus of 480 sentences, eight degradation
conditions, and a five-level noise severity sweep, covering five recognition
systems from three architectural families, two cascade translation paths,
and two end-to-end speech translation paths, all accessed through their
public interfaces on free service quotas.

The measurements support five conclusions. First, the architectural families
of commercial recognisers differ sharply and systematically in noise
robustness: LLM-based recognisers degrade at 0.34 to 0.44 percentage points per dB in Mandarin against 1.70 for a specialised streaming recogniser, and the family gap exceeds a factor of two in pooled error rate at 0~dB.
Second, robustness is condition- and language-specific rather than uniform:
narrowbanding, coding, packet loss, clipping, and tempo changes at deployed
severities are individually negligible for all systems, while 0~dB babble
nearly destroys English recognition for every system and leaves the best Mandarin recognition almost intact. Third, the quality of a speech
translation cascade under degradation is governed by its recognition
frontend, and the relationship is linear and predictable, with a
pass-through of 0.47 to 0.66 BLEU points per percentage point of recognition
error at a correlation above 0.98 across twelve conditions. Fourth,
end-to-end speech translation is not automatically more robust than a
cascade: the most resilient translation path measured is a dedicated
streaming end-to-end service, while the omni-modal end-to-end path is both
the slowest and, under severe noise, the most fragile. Fifth, black-box
evaluation carries specific methodological obligations that this benchmark
operationalises, including dual-reference scoring to control machine-reference
affinity, exact-transcript corpora to enable sub-percent measurement, and
the reporting of response completeness alongside error rates.

The benchmark recipe, including the corpus, the seeded degradation pipeline,
all model responses, and the analysis code, is released so that the
measurement can be re-executed as providers update their services. The released artefacts are versioned alongside the service
identifiers and access dates of every system, so that future re-runs can
attribute score movements either to provider-side model updates or to
changes in the benchmark itself, a distinction that periodic evaluation of
moving targets requires. Together with the zero-cost execution protocol
described in Section~\ref{sec:methods}, the release is intended to make
robustness re-measurement a routine maintenance activity for speech
deployments rather than a one-off research exercise. As
speech intelligence continues to migrate into provider-operated black-box
systems, controlled measurement of this kind, repeated over time and across
providers, offers the community a practical substitute for the training-set
access that classical robustness research assumed, and offers integrators
the quantitative basis that architectural intuition alone cannot supply.
