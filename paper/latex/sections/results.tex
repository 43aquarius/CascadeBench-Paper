\section{Results and analysis}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Clean baseline and the main degradation grid}
\label{sec:results:main}

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports sentence-level error rates on the main grid for
the five recognition systems, and Fig.~\ref{fig:heatmap} visualises the same
data split by language. On clean synthetic speech the three foundation-model
recognisers are close to their scoring floor, with error rates between 0.3
and 0.5 percent, while the specialised streaming recogniser operates at 0.56
percent on Chinese and 3.99 percent on English, reflecting its primarily
Mandarin training. The telephone-band specialist reaches 1.86 percent on its
native language. The clean-condition figures establish that the comparisons
under degradation are not confounded by differences in clean accuracy of
comparable magnitude.

Three regularities stand out in the grid. First, babble noise at 0~dB is by
far the most destructive condition for every system, but the magnitude of
the collapse separates the architectural families sharply. The specialised
streaming recogniser reaches a pooled error rate of 58.5 percent and the
telephone-band specialist 73.2 percent, whereas the two LLM-ASR systems
remain at 25 to 28 percent and the omni model at 31.8 percent. At matched
severity, the foundation-model recognisers commit roughly half the error of
the specialised systems, and the difference is significant at the sentence
level: in Chinese at 0~dB, Qwen-Audio 3.1 errs at 6.3 percent against 41.6
percent for the streaming specialist (Wilcoxon $p<0.001$, $n=240$).

Second, the effect of noise is strongly language-asymmetric in a way that
differs by family. For the LLM-ASR systems, Chinese recognition at 0~dB is
nearly immune, at 6.3 percent for Qwen-Audio 3.1 and 13.1 percent for Qwen3
ASR, while their English error rates at the same severity reach 50.2 and 37.1
percent. The specialised streaming recogniser suffers heavily in both
languages, 41.6 percent in Chinese and 75.5 percent in English. The babble
mixture is language-mixed, so the asymmetry cannot be attributed to
language-matched interference alone; a plausible partial explanation is that
the tonal structure of Mandarin provides complementary cues that survive
energetic masking, a hypothesis consistent with the general observation that
Mandarin recognition degrades more slowly than English under stationary
interference~\cite{virtanen2012overview}.

Third, the non-noise degradations expose narrow specialisation rather than
uniform fragility. Narrowbanding to the telephone band costs the LLM-ASR
systems almost nothing, 0.3 to 0.6 percent, and the omni model is essentially
unaffected at 0.1 percent, whereas the telephone-band specialist shows no
measurable home-field advantage over the generalist systems in its own band.
Reverberation, by contrast, is disproportionately expensive for the
telephone-band specialist at 18.2 percent, an order of magnitude above every
other system, which suggests that its front-end processing is tuned for
direct-path telephone speech. Codec compression, packet loss, clipping, and
tempo distortion at the chosen severities are individually mild for all
systems, raising error rates by at most one percentage point over clean
speech. This does not make them harmless: Section~\ref{sec:results:cascade}
shows that even sub-point recognition changes propagate measurably into
translation quality, and the conditions were deliberately calibrated to
deployed-but-aggressive levels rather than to failure-inducing extremes.

Two further behaviours deserve mention because they are invisible in
conventional aggregate reporting. The numeral-and-entity domain is five times
more error-prone than the other domains for Qwen-Audio 3.1 on clean speech,
at 1.25 against 0.25 percent, whereas Qwen3 ASR shows no such penalty, which
indicates that inverse text normalisation rather than acoustics dominates
that gap. More strikingly, Qwen-Audio 3.1 is the only system that ever
declines to answer: on 5.2 percent of the 0~dB utterances, concentrated in
English at 9.6 percent of that subset, it returns an empty transcript, and
these refusals are scored as complete failures in Table~\ref{tab:main}. The
other four systems always emit a hypothesis, however wrong. For a downstream
consumer this is an operationally meaningful difference between a wrong
answer and no answer, and it is the kind of behavioural divergence that only
black-box evaluation at controlled severity can surface.

The remaining conditions merit condition-specific remarks because their
effects are small in aggregate but structured in detail. Reverberation with
a reverberation time of 0.5~s raises the specialised streaming recogniser to
3.2 percent pooled, but the effect concentrates in English, where it reaches
5.1 percent against 1.3 percent in Chinese, and the paired difference
against the LLM-ASR systems remains significant in both languages
(Wilcoxon $p<0.01$ in Chinese, $p<0.001$ in English). The omni model shows
an idiosyncratic signature under reverberation and clipping, at 1.1 and 1.0
percent, roughly triple its clean error rate, which suggests that its audio
encoder is more sensitive to spectral colouration and non-linear distortion
than the dedicated recognisers even though it dominates under noise. Codec
compression at 8~kbit/s leaves every system within 0.3 percentage points of
its clean score, confirming that deployed neural codecs are essentially
transparent to modern recognisers at this bitrate. Packet loss at 15 percent
and clipping at the 0.3 threshold behave as mild aggregate stressors, and
tempo distortion at 1.15 times is fully transparent to every system, which
is expected because time-scale modification preserves the spectral envelope
that recognisers rely on. The practical reading of these patterns is that a
service integrator who cannot control the acoustic environment should
prioritise noise mitigation far above codec or transport tuning, since the
latter contribute almost nothing to the error budget at realistic operating
points.

\subsection{Stratified analysis by domain}
\label{sec:results:domain}

Because the corpus is balanced by construction, error rates can be
decomposed by content domain without re-weighting. On clean audio the
technical domain is the most error-prone for the specialised streaming
recogniser at 3.3 percent, consistent with its terminological density, while
the conversation domain is the easiest for every system, below 0.1 percent for the Qwen-Audio recogniser. The numerals-and-entities domain shows the
clearest family split on clean speech: the two LLM-ASR systems differ by an
order of magnitude on it, at 1.2 percent for Qwen-Audio against 0.15 percent
for Qwen3-ASR, which as noted in Section~\ref{sec:results:main} points to
inverse text normalisation rather than acoustics as the separating factor.
Under 0~dB noise the domain differences compress for every system: the
spread across domains narrows to about nine points for the Qwen-Audio
recogniser and to twelve points for the specialised streaming recogniser,
and the ordering changes, with the technical domain becoming the most
difficult for both. The compression indicates that under severe energetic
masking the bottleneck shifts from lexical and normalisation knowledge,
which distinguishes systems on clean speech, to the acoustic recovery
problem, which is common to all systems and dominates the error budget.

\subsection{Scaling with noise severity}
\label{sec:results:snr}

Fig.~\ref{fig:snr} shows error rate as a function of signal-to-noise ratio
on the 144-sentence severity sweep, and Table~\ref{tab:slopes} reports the
fitted degradation gradients. The relation is approximately linear over the
measured 20 to 0~dB range for all systems, which makes the slope a compact
summary of noise robustness. The specialised streaming recogniser loses 1.70
percentage points of Chinese accuracy per dB and 3.08 points per dB in
English, whereas the LLM-ASR systems lose 0.34 to 0.44 points per dB in Chinese and 1.35 to 1.57 in English. In relative terms the specialised system degrades four to five times faster in Chinese and about twice as fast in English. The omni model, measured on the main grid points only,
tracks the LLM-ASR family. The crossover structure visible in
Fig.~\ref{fig:snr} carries the practical content: above roughly 5~dB the
systems are within a point of each other and choice can be driven by latency
or cost, but below 5~dB the families separate rapidly, and below 0~dB no
system delivers usable English transcription, with error rates between 37
and 76 percent. The sweep also shows that the separation is gradual
rather than threshold-like. Between 20 and 10~dB every system stays within
one percentage point of its clean score, so the mild-noise regime is
effectively free of robustness concerns for all families. Between 10 and
5~dB the specialised system begins to move first, and by 5~dB the family
ordering is already established in both languages, which means that
robustness differences can be detected at moderate severity without
stressing any system to failure. The practical implication for procurement
testing is that a single 5~dB babble probe suffices to rank candidate
recognition services by noise robustness in this measurement, since the
ordering at 5~dB matches the ordering at 0~dB in both languages for every
pair of systems compared.

\subsection{Cascade versus end-to-end translation under degradation}
\label{sec:results:cascade}

Fig.~\ref{fig:cascade} compares the four speech translation paths across the
main grid, and Table~\ref{tab:translation} reports the corresponding scores.
On clean audio the two cascades lose almost nothing relative to their
translation oracle, the text translation engine applied to perfect
transcripts, which scores 55.5 BLEU against the primary reference. The
cascade built on Qwen-Audio 3.1 starts at 53.9 BLEU, the cascade built on
the streaming specialist at 51.5, and the end-to-end omni path scores
highest at 68.1 with the streaming LiveTranslate service at 59.8. Under
degradation the ordering and the magnitudes change dramatically. At 0~dB the
Qwen-Audio cascade drops 9.9 BLEU points, the streaming-specialist cascade
collapses by 35.7 points to 15.8 BLEU, the omni end-to-end path loses 22.5
points, and the streaming translation service loses 9.3 points from a lower starting level.

Two conclusions follow. The first concerns the frontend hypothesis. The
translation quality of a cascade under acoustic degradation is governed
almost entirely by the robustness of its recognition stage: swapping the
frontend from the streaming specialist to the LLM recogniser at 0~dB is
worth 28 BLEU points on the same backend, far more than any architectural
change downstream. The second qualifies the common expectation that
end-to-end systems avoid cascade error propagation and are therefore more
robust. At 0~dB the omni end-to-end path loses a third of its BLEU score,
more than the well-frontended cascade loses at 18 percent, and its absolute
score falls below that cascade. The streaming LiveTranslate service is the
most degradation-resilient of the four, losing 16 percent at 0~dB, which is
consistent with the behaviour of its internal frontend recogniser
documented in Section~\ref{sec:results:frontend}. End-to-end designs do not
inherit robustness automatically; they inherit the robustness of whatever
acoustic frontend their provider trained internally, which in this
measurement is better than the specialised streaming ASR but not
automatically better than the best cascade.

The condition-wise structure of Fig.~\ref{fig:cascade} adds three nuances to
this picture. First, apart from the 0~dB point the cascade built on the LLM
recogniser is essentially flat across the entire grid, moving within one
BLEU point of its clean score under every other condition, which mirrors the
recognition flatness of Table~\ref{tab:main} and confirms that the
non-noise degradations are, at deployed severities, engineering noise rather
than failure modes for a robust frontend. Second, the streaming translation
service shows the same flatness with a single exception: its narrowband
score of 60.1 sits marginally above its clean score, and its packet-loss and
tempo scores are likewise within half a point of clean, so its 0~dB loss is
the only substantive degradation it suffers. Third, the omni end-to-end path
exhibits a small but consistent anomaly: its packet-loss and tempo scores,
at 71.1 and 70.0, exceed its clean score of 68.1. Inspection of the outputs
suggests that under these mild distortions the model produces slightly more
concise translations that happen to align better with the reference style,
rather than genuinely better translations; the effect is within the
reference-sensitivity margin documented in Section~\ref{sec:results:dual}
and is a further argument for treating small absolute differences between
heterogeneous systems with caution.

Decomposing the 0~dB collapse by direction sharpens the frontend story. The
specialist cascade loses 26 BLEU points in the Chinese-to-English direction
but 46 points in the English-to-Chinese direction, falling to 8.7 BLEU,
because its English recognition, at 75.5 percent error, destroys the source
representation before the translation stage operates. The LLM-frontend
cascade degrades far less and nearly symmetrically, losing 7.0 points in one
direction and 15.0 in the other. The streaming translation service loses
almost nothing in the Chinese-to-English direction at 0~dB, at 55.5 against
55.9 clean, but 18 points in the other direction, which parallels the
language asymmetry of its internal frontend. Direction-symmetric
deployment claims should therefore be checked per direction rather than
pooled.

\subsection{Reference sensitivity of translation scoring}
\label{sec:results:dual}

Table~\ref{tab:translation} also reports scores against the secondary
reference, the oracle cascade output. The two references invert the apparent
ordering of the systems on clean audio. Against the independent LLM
reference the omni end-to-end path leads at 68.1 BLEU, exceeding even the
text-translation oracle at 55.5. Against the oracle reference the same
end-to-end path scores 48.5 while the Qwen-Audio cascade scores 89.6, since
the cascade and the reference generator are the same model. The inversion
quantifies a hazard for any evaluation that scores machine translation
against machine-generated references: absolute scores, and even the identity
of the apparent best system, depend on which model family generated the
reference. Two safeguards make the present conclusions robust to this
effect. Degradation losses are computed within a system against a fixed
reference, so the percentage losses reported above do not depend on the
choice, and the ordering of losses at 0~dB, at 69, 33, 18, and 16 percent
for the four paths, is preserved under both references. We recommend that
future black-box translation evaluations report at least one reference from
a model family disjoint from every system under test, and state the family
relationships explicitly.

\subsection{Error pass-through from recognition to translation}
\label{sec:results:cac}

Fig.~\ref{fig:cac} relates the BLEU loss of the two cascades to their mean
recognition error rate across all twelve operating conditions. The
relationship is strikingly linear: the Pearson correlation between
recognition error and translation loss is 0.988 for the Qwen-Audio cascade
and 0.999 for the specialist cascade over twelve points spanning 0.1 to 59
percent recognition error. The fitted pass-through slopes are 0.47 BLEU
points per percentage point of recognition error for the Qwen-Audio cascade
and 0.66 for the specialist cascade. The steeper slope for the specialised
frontend reflects the composition of its errors under severe noise, which
include whole-utterance failures where the translation engine receives
little or no usable signal, whereas the LLM recogniser's residual errors
under moderate degradation are concentrated in individual words that the
translation engine can often route around. For engineering purposes the
rule is simple and accurate in this measurement: every percentage point of
recognition error costs about half a BLEU point downstream, and under
conditions that produce outright recognition failures the cost approaches
two thirds of a point. A service integrator can therefore estimate the
translation consequence of a candidate frontend from recognition error
measurements alone, without running the full pipeline.

\subsection{The streaming translation frontend as a recogniser}
\label{sec:results:frontend}

The streaming translation service exposes its internal recognition output,
which allows its frontend, a specialised streaming recogniser, to be scored
as a sixth system on the severity sweep. Its clean-condition error rate of
0.9 percent is comparable to the other specialised systems, but under noise
it behaves markedly better, reaching 18.2 percent at 0~dB, against 25 to 28
percent for the LLM-ASR systems and 58.5 percent for the general-purpose
streaming specialist, and it does so at the streaming latency of its class.
This is a noteworthy observation for practitioners: the most noise-robust
recogniser measured in this study is not directly purchasable as a
recognition service but is embedded inside a translation product, which
suggests that providers optimise internal streaming frontends for exactly
the acoustic conditions where end-to-end translation must survive.

\subsection{Sentence-level variability and latency}
\label{sec:results:latency}

Fig.~\ref{fig:dist} shows the sentence-level error distributions for the
strongest LLM recogniser and the specialised streaming recogniser on five
representative conditions. The distributions are strongly right-skewed:
under moderate degradation most sentences remain nearly correct while a
tail of sentences fails completely, which is why the confidence intervals in
Table~\ref{tab:main} are wide relative to their means. Under 0~dB babble the
median sentence is still recognised reasonably well by the LLM systems while
the upper quartile fails outright, a heterogeneity that aggregate error
rates conceal and that matters for applications with per-utterance quality
requirements. The skew also explains a statistical property visible
throughout Table~
ef{tab:main}: the confidence intervals of the LLM systems
under moderate degradation are wide relative to their means because a small
number of complete failures, including the refusal cases, coexist with a
large mass of near-perfect transcripts. For a system operator this shape
matters more than the mean, since the tail sentences are the ones that
generate complaints and downstream errors, and the fraction of sentences
above a given error threshold is a more actionable deployment metric than
the average. On the 0~dB condition, the fraction of English sentences with
error rates above 50 percent is 83 percent for the specialised streaming
recogniser against 36 percent for Qwen3-ASR, while in Chinese the same
threshold is exceeded by 33 percent of sentences for the specialised system
against 3 percent for Qwen-Audio, so the family gap in tail behaviour is
considerably larger than the gap in means.

produces its translation in a single response. The streaming translation
service completes in under a second. Latency therefore reinforces rather
than reverses the accuracy picture: the paths that degrade least under
noise, the well-frontended cascade and the streaming translation service,
are also among the fastest, while the slowest path, the omni end-to-end
model, is the most fragile under severe noise in this measurement.
