\title{Error Propagation in Cascaded Speech Translation under Acoustic Channel
Degradations: A Black-Box Benchmark of Commercial Speech Intelligence APIs}

\author[addr1]{Anonymous Author}
\address[addr1]{Affiliation withheld for double-blind review}

\begin{abstract}
Modern speech intelligence services are increasingly assembled from black-box
cloud APIs, in which an automatic speech recognition (ASR) system feeds a
machine translation (MT) engine in a cascade, or are replaced by end-to-end
speech translation (E2E ST) models. The acoustic channels that carry speech to
these systems are rarely clean, yet the sensitivity of each architectural
choice to realistic channel impairments is difficult to assess because
commercial systems can only be queried through their interfaces. This paper
presents CascadeBench, a black-box benchmark that quantifies how eight
canonical acoustic channel degradations, including multi-talker babble noise
at five signal-to-noise ratios, room reverberation, telephone narrowbanding,
low-bitrate coding, packet loss, amplitude clipping, and tempo distortion,
propagate through the recognition and translation stages of commercial speech
APIs. A stratified bilingual corpus of 480 Chinese and English sentences was
synthesised with multiple neural voices, degraded in a fully controlled and
reproducible pipeline, and processed by five recognition systems spanning three
architectural families, together with two cascade and two end-to-end speech
translation paths. Results show that large-language-model ASR services degrade several times more slowly under noise than specialised streaming models, with degradation slopes of 0.3 to 0.4 percentage points per dB against 1.7 for the specialised system in Mandarin, but a dedicated telephone-band model remains
competitive only in its home band. At a signal-to-noise ratio of 0~dB the
strongest cascade loses 18 percent of its BLEU score while the weakest loses 69
percent, and end-to-end speech translation proves more fragile than a
well-frontend cascade. Across twelve operating conditions, translation quality
declines linearly with the recognition error rate, with a pass-through slope of
0.47 to 0.66 BLEU points per percentage point of recognition error and a
correlation above 0.98, which provides system integrators with a simple
engineering rule for estimating translation losses. All experiments were
executed on free-tier API quotas, and the complete benchmark recipe, including
the corpus, perturbation code, and per-sentence transcripts, is released for
reproduction.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and code availability}
The full benchmark recipe, including the sentence corpus, the perturbation
implementations with fixed random seeds, all per-sentence model transcripts and
translations, and the analysis scripts, will be made publicly available at a
repository link provided upon acceptance. The data can also be reconstructed
end to end from the documentation in this paper using free-tier API quotas.
