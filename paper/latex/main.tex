\title{Error Propagation in Cascaded Speech Translation under Acoustic Channel
Degradations: A Black-Box Case Study of Commercial Speech Intelligence APIs}

\author[addr1]{Chang Tan\corref{cor1}}
\ead{aquars43@foxmail.com}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\address[addr1]{Dalian University of Technology, Dalian, China}

\begin{abstract}
Cloud speech services are black boxes, yet their acoustic channels are
rarely clean. CascadeBench is a seeded black-box
benchmark measuring how fourteen degradations from seven acoustic
families propagate through commercial speech APIs: five recognition systems
in three product classes of one provider, three cascaded and two end-to-end
translation paths, on a 480-sentence bilingual corpus with an eight-level
babble sweep. Recognition error rises
knee-shaped with noise: audio-LLM systems tolerate
2.2--2.5~dB (Mandarin) and 4.4--4.8~dB (English) more noise than the
specialised streaming system before ten percent error, then
collapse into insertion-heavy hallucination above 100
percent error. Cascade loss follows an empirical pass-through of
0.4 to 0.5 BLEU points per percentage point of recognition error; a
nine-condition re-fit on a second vendor's engine retains the linear form
with coefficients about a quarter lower (0.32, 0.56); engine-independence is
not established. A 150-utterance real-speech validation
reproduces the knee and hallucination structure, finds
measured-RIR rooms far milder than the synthetic stress case, and
bounds the audio-LLM advantage to the near-knee regime; an open-weights
recogniser crosses ten percent in the specialist
band (Mandarin) and between the classes (English), so the advantage is not
reproduced on an open encoder--decoder, and its
class-versus-vendor provenance remains open. End-to-end
translation is not automatically more robust: the omni-modal path, the
most fragile, trails the LLM-frontend cascades with a
reference-dependent margin. Three-reference scoring with one family-disjoint
reference shows relative-loss rankings stable
up to a sub-point same-engine inversion. Recipe,
corpus, degraded audio, and 43{,}773 logged API responses are released.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and code availability}
The complete benchmark is publicly released at
\url{https://github.com/43aquarius/CascadeBench-Paper-AppliedAcoustics}:
the sentence corpus, the seeded perturbation implementations, the full
degraded audio tree, all per-sentence model transcripts and translations
(43{,}773 logged API responses), the analysis scripts, and the LaTeX source
of this manuscript. Every number and figure in the paper is regenerated
from the released logs by the released analysis scripts. Extended numeric
tables (complete pairwise significance results, severity-sweep numerics,
refusal tabulations, and the complete real-speech, Whisper, measured-RIR,
and second-backend results) are provided as supplementary material.
Because the
journal operates single-anonymised review, in which author identity is
visible to reviewers, the public link is given directly; an anonymised
snapshot is additionally provided with the submission.
