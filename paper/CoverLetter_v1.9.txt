Cover Letter — Computer Speech & Language submission

Dear Editors,

Please find enclosed our manuscript "Error Propagation in Cascaded Speech
Translation under Acoustic Channel Degradations: A Black-Box Case Study of
Commercial Speech Intelligence APIs", which we submit as a full-length
original research article.

Commercial speech APIs are consumed as black boxes, yet the acoustic channels
feeding them are rarely clean. The manuscript introduces CascadeBench, a fully
seeded black-box benchmark that measures how fourteen acoustic degradation
conditions propagate through the recognition and translation services of one
provider (five recognition systems in three product classes, three cascaded
and two end-to-end translation paths), on a 480-sentence bilingual corpus
with an eight-level babble sweep. The paper reports a knee-shaped error
response to noise, a hallucination regime below 0 dB, an empirical linear
pass-through from recognition error to translation loss, and a
three-reference scoring protocol that separates genuine degradation from
machine-reference affinity.

We believe the manuscript sits squarely within the scope of Computer Speech &
Language. Its subject matter is the robustness of automatic speech
recognition and spoken-language translation systems — how recognition error
behaves under acoustic channel degradation, how that error propagates into
cascade translation, and how end-to-end and cascade architectures compare
under identical stress — which are core speech and language processing
questions. Its methodology is a systematic, fully seeded benchmark with
released logs, open-weights replication (Whisper small and large-v3-turbo on
the full real-babble ladder), a second-vendor translation engine (GLM) as
both a family-disjoint scoring reference and an alternative cascade backend,
and Monte Carlo uncertainty quantification calibrated against directly
measured generation noise — the kind of reproducible, measurement-driven
evaluation study the journal's readership builds upon. We are not aware of a
comparable public, sentence-level account of error propagation through
deployed commercial speech translation services, and the benchmark itself
(corpus, perturbation recipes, degraded audio, 45,288 logged API responses,
and analysis scripts) is released for reuse.

The measurement is anchored beyond the vendor's own stack by three
independent validations: (1) a 150-utterance real-speech validation (AISHELL-1
and LibriSpeech test speakers, real multi-talker babble and transport noise,
and measured room impulse responses from the MIT IR Survey) that reproduces
the knee and hallucination structure and calibrates the synthetic
reverberation condition as an extreme direct-to-reverberant stress case;
(2) open-weights recognisers executed locally on the same real-speech
conditions, which show that the Mandarin-insertion / English-deletion failure
structure replicates outside the vendor while noise-tolerance levels do not,
and which localise the non-vendor knee on the full six-level babble ladder —
in the specialist band in Mandarin and between the two product classes in
English (a placement shown robust to traditional-to-simplified script
mismatch by a sensitivity re-scoring) — so the audio-LLM robustness
advantage is not reproduced on an open encoder–decoder of the previous
generation, and whether it is a class or a vendor property remains open; and
(3) the second-vendor translation engine used both as a family-disjoint
scoring reference and as an alternative cascade backend re-translating all
nine real-speech conditions, which retains the linear form of the
pass-through relation on a nine-condition re-fit with coefficients about a
quarter lower — a gap whose significance is established against generation
noise that is measured directly by regenerating the reference and the
mild-condition candidates under the identical protocol (1,515 regenerations,
all released) — and bounds reference-affinity effects on the end-to-end
comparison. These are described in Sections 4.4–4.8 and the supplementary
material.

All data and code are publicly available: the complete benchmark (corpus,
seeded degradation pipeline, degraded audio tree, 45,288 logged API
responses, analysis scripts, and LaTeX source) is released at
https://github.com/43aquarius/CascadeBench-Paper, and the manuscript carries
a data availability statement with the link. Because the journal operates a
single-anonymised review process, the public link is given directly in the
manuscript, and an anonymised repository snapshot is additionally provided
with the submission. The work uses only free service quotas accessed through
public interfaces and involves no human or animal subjects. The authors
declare no competing interests, no specific funding was received for this
research, and a declaration of generative AI use in the writing process is
included in the manuscript before the reference list.

This manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under
consideration by any other journal. Reviewer suggestions are provided in the
submission system as requested by the guide for authors. We look forward to
the reviewers' assessments.
