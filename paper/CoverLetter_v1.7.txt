Cover Letter — Applied Acoustics submission

Dear Editors,

Please find enclosed our manuscript "Error Propagation in Cascaded Speech
Translation under Acoustic Channel Degradations: A Black-Box Case Study of
Commercial Speech Intelligence APIs", which we submit as a full-length
original research article.

Commercial speech APIs are consumed as black boxes, yet the acoustic channels
feeding them are rarely clean. The manuscript introduces CascadeBench, a fully
seeded black-box benchmark that measures how fourteen acoustic degradation
conditions propagate through the recognition and translation services of one
provider (five recognition systems in three product classes, three cascaded
and two end-to-end translation paths), on a 480-sentence bilingual corpus
with an eight-level babble sweep. The paper reports a knee-shaped error
response to noise, a hallucination regime below 0 dB, an empirical linear
pass-through from recognition error to translation loss, and a
three-reference scoring protocol that separates genuine degradation from
machine-reference affinity. We believe this quantitative, measurement-driven
treatment of deployed speech services, with direct implications for
integrators of speech interfaces in acoustically degraded environments, fits
the journal's scope of applied acoustics with engineering impact.

The measurement is anchored beyond the vendor's own stack by three
independent validations: (1) a 150-utterance real-speech validation (AISHELL-1
and LibriSpeech test speakers, real multi-talker babble and transport noise,
and measured room impulse responses from the MIT IR Survey) that reproduces
the knee and hallucination structure and calibrates the synthetic
reverberation condition as an extreme direct-to-reverberant stress case;
(2) open-weights recognisers (Whisper small and the 809M-parameter
Whisper large-v3-turbo) executed locally on the same real-speech
conditions, which show that the Mandarin-insertion / English-deletion
failure structure replicates outside the vendor while noise-tolerance
levels do not, and which localise the non-vendor knee on the full
six-level babble ladder -- in the specialist band in Mandarin and between
the two product classes in English, so the audio-LLM robustness advantage
is provider-specific rather than generic; and (3) a second-vendor translation engine
(GLM, Z.ai) used both as a family-disjoint scoring reference and as an
alternative cascade backend re-translating all nine real-speech conditions,
which reproduces the linear form of the pass-through relation on a
nine-condition re-fit (with an engine-dependent coefficient) and bounds
reference-affinity effects on the end-to-end comparison. These are described
in Sections 4.4-4.8 and the supplementary material.

All data and code are publicly available: the complete benchmark (corpus,
seeded degradation pipeline, degraded audio tree, 43,773 logged API
responses, analysis scripts, and LaTeX source) is released at
https://github.com/43aquarius/CascadeBench-Paper-AppliedAcoustics, and the
manuscript carries a data availability statement with the link. The work
uses only free service quotas accessed through public interfaces and
involves no human or animal subjects. The authors declare no competing
interests. A declaration of generative AI use in the writing process is
included in the manuscript before the reference list.

This manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under
consideration by any other journal. We look forward to the reviewers'
assessments.
